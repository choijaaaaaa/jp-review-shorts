This is Beryl's macadamia chocolate, sitting on a shelf at Don Quijote in Osaka — but flip the box over and there's a country name you wouldn't expect.

Right next to "Made in Malaysia" sits a round Halal certification stamp.

Beryl's got its start in Malaysia back in 1995, and it's since grown into a brand sold across eighteen countries and regions.

It only reached Japanese supermarket shelves in November 2025 — and twenty-eight of those stores set up a special corner just for Halal-certified chocolate.

That stamp on the box is Malaysia's Halal standard, MS 1500 — basically a signal Muslim travelers can shop with confidence.

Japanese reviewers say the chocolate coating around the almond is pretty thick, with a strong milk flavor.

A few add it leans sweet, so pairing it with something like coffee balances things out.

One reviewer admitted they weren't expecting much from a Malaysian product — until they learned it's actually a premium gift chocolate back home, even served as an in-flight snack.

Bottom line: Beryl's macadamia chocolate looks ordinary, but it's actually a Halal-certified, premium Malaysian brand — that's the real story here.
