This bandage was sitting on a shelf at Don Quijote in Osaka, and its name actually hides a clever meaning.

The green box has "flexible fit" printed right across the front, with sizes running from small to large.

Careleaves is a mashup of "care" and "leaf" — a nod to the bandage's own leaf-like shape.

It also sounds just like the word "leave," as in wishing your wound would leave you alone, and that meaning got folded into the name too.

It launched in 1997, after three years of development.

Back then, most Japanese bandages were stiff and didn't breathe well, so the brand switched to something new: a high-density urethane nonwoven fabric.

That fabric stretches both lengthwise and widthwise, so it's built to move with you even when you bend a finger.

Reviewers back that up — one said it "stretches enough to bend comfortably and doesn't feel sticky once you peel it off," while another said it held up on joints that bend constantly.

Some people even use it on their eyelids instead of double-eyelid tape, though one reviewer warned that wearing it there too long can stretch the skin.

Bottom line: Careleaves clearly thought through everything from the name to the material, so wherever you use it, just don't leave it on one spot for too long.
