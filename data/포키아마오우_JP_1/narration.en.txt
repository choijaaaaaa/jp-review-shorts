This Pocky is from a Don Quijote in Osaka, and it's not a name any random Fukuoka strawberry gets to use.

The box has both a "Japan-exclusive" stamp and a "Kyushu specialty" stamp, and the name "Amaou" itself is already special.

It's an acronym from four words — red, round, big, delicious — chosen out of 1,913 entries in a 2002 public naming contest in Fukuoka, and only strawberries that meet the standard get to carry that name.

The size alone stands out — people say things like "this Pocky is seriously huge, it's shocking" — and the flavor is a pale pink, mellow strawberry-milk taste with low acidity.

The crisp pretzel stick has a biscuit-like crunch, which pairs nicely with the strawberry cream in every bite.

That said, if you prefer more tartness, some people say they'd actually pick the regular strawberry Pocky instead.

Sweetness-wise it's 12 to 13 Brix, which is actually pretty average — but the ratio of large berries over 20 grams is almost double older varieties, and this Pocky uses 8 percent real Amaou pulp.

Bottom line: the size and real fruit content are genuinely satisfying, though how much you like it depends on how much tartness you're looking for.
