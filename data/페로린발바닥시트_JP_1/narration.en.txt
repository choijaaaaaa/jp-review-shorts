Korea's Daiso also sells a Japanese mugwort foot-patch, but at Don Quijote in Osaka, the same lineup of brand splits it into four different scents.

The box has a big "doro-doro" (murky/gunky) label and a large photo of a patch that's turned dark brown.

The ingredients are chamomile, mugwort, wood vinegar, and houttuynia — Daiso's version only has mugwort and houttuynia extract, while this Perorin chamomile version adds wood vinegar on top.

The wood vinegar absorbs sweat, which is what actually turns the patch brown — Japanese verification blogs have already confirmed it's from sweat, not toxins.

People report their skin actually getting smoother and less rough, though how long it takes to notice varies a lot — anywhere from one week to ten weeks.

The one thing everyone seems to agree on: it's convenient because all you do is wear it, nothing else.

Bottom line: results vary person to person, but the sheer simplicity of just slipping it on makes it worth trying.
