This is Starbucks' Sakura Strawberry Latte, found at Don Quijote in Osaka — and it's not something you'll find in Korea's official Starbucks At Home lineup at all.

The box is covered in cheerful cherry blossom and strawberry art, but there's not a single drop of real fruit juice in it — every bit of that flavor comes from flavoring.

This latte isn't sold year-round either. It only shows up for a few weeks around February 15th, as part of Starbucks' spring lineup.

When it first launched, so many people said the sakura scent was overpowering that Starbucks reworked the whole flavor blend in 2023 and relaunched it.

Even after that, opinions are still split.

Some say the sakura scent hit way too hard and honestly made them feel a little sick, or that it was so strong they had to water it down just to finish the cup.

Others say they crave it every single year — the sakura barely registers for them, but they love how the strawberry plays off the coffee's bitterness.

One person bought it as a souvenir for a friend abroad and noticed it stacked front and center on the shelf; another said they wanted to buy it again, only to find it already gone.

The actual review average lands in the high 3s out of 5 — 71% gave it four or five stars, but 19% gave it just one or two, so reactions genuinely go both ways.

Korea's official Starbucks At Home menu carries matcha latte, but never this flavor — anything you find in Korea is someone's personal import from Japan.

So here's the bottom line: it disappears every year by March, it's never sold through official channels in Korea, and love it or hate it, the Starbucks Sakura Strawberry Latte is a flavor you can only catch in Japan for a few weeks a year.
