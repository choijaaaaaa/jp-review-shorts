Found this umeboshi sheet candy in the snack aisle at Don Quijote in Osaka.

It looks like a flat stick of gum, but it's got a little give when you press it — like someone genuinely flattened a real plum into a sheet.

Inaba Peanuts, the company behind it, has been making sweets since 1918. That's over a hundred years in business.

The ingredient list stays short: plum, sugar, salt, a bit of starch. You'll find it at basically any 7-Eleven or FamilyMart.

Nothing like this exists back home — Korean plum products mean pickled plums or plum syrup, not a pressed candy sheet.

Supposedly, about one in ten thousand sheets comes out heart-shaped.

One food blogger said the cute packaging lulled her in, then the sourness hit way harder than she expected.

Another reviewer described the first bite as a little powdery, but called the sweet-sour plum flavor addictive — texture-wise, kind of like vinegared kelp, sweet first, sour on the finish.

Not every review lands that way, though. A few people said the flavor felt off and the whole thing was a letdown.

Bottom line: this Umeboshi Sheet hides real plum sourness behind a cute wrapper, so whether you end up loving it really depends on your tolerance for sour.
